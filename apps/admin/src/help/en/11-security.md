# Security

In this section you will learn how to keep your admin panel out of the wrong hands: a strong password, two-step verification, team members and their roles, how sessions end, what happens after failed sign-ins, how secret keys are stored, and what you can monitor in the audit log. It all lives on the **Users & security** and **Audit log** pages.

![Users & security page](help/en/security.png)

Your panel holds customer details, orders and money movements. That makes your panel account more valuable than an email account. The good news: the most important protections (a password and two-step verification) take five minutes to set up.

> [!TIP]
> Ninety percent of security comes down to three habits: a long, unique password, two-step verification, and giving team members only the permissions they need.

## Password rules {#sifre-kurallari}

* It must be at least **12 characters** long (at most 256).
* Choose a password you use nowhere else. A phrase of three or four words (`blue-cup-night-rain`) is long and memorable.
* We recommend using a password manager.
* Your password is not stored as plain text on the server; it is stored irreversibly scrambled (a PBKDF2-SHA256 hash). That is why nobody, not even an admin, can see your password.

**To change your password:** in **Users & security → Change password**, enter your current password, then your new password twice. When the password changes, **your sessions on all other devices are signed out**; the session you are in stays open.

> [!NOTE]
> If you forget your password, there is no "forgot password" link in the panel. The store owner can set a new temporary password for you (see [Users and roles](#kullanicilar-ve-roller) below). If the only account is the store owner's and the password is forgotten, recovery needs technical intervention; so keep your password somewhere safe.

## Two-step verification {#iki-adimli-dogrulama}

[Two-step verification (TOTP)](term:totp) keeps your account from being entered even if your password is stolen. When you sign in, besides your password you also enter a 6-digit code, generated by an app on your phone, that changes every 30 seconds.

**Who must use it?** Two-step verification is mandatory for store owner and admin accounts; it is optional for staff. If you have not set it up yet, the panel guides you through it.

### Setup {#kurulum}

**Before you start:** Install an authenticator app on your phone (such as Google Authenticator, Microsoft Authenticator, Authy or 1Password). Whichever one you use will work.

1. In the **Users & security → Two-factor authentication (TOTP)** card, press the **Set up 2FA** button.
2. A **QR code** appears on screen. Scan it with the app on your phone.
3. If you cannot scan the QR code, click the **Can't scan? Enter key manually** link; type the key that appears into the app by hand.
4. A new entry called "OpenShelf" appears in the app and starts showing a 6-digit code.
5. Enter this code in the **Code** field and press **Enable**.
6. You see the **Enabled** badge on the card.

From now on, this code is requested after your password at every sign-in.

### Signing in {#giris-yaparken}

1. Enter your email and password and sign in.
2. The "Two-factor code" screen appears; enter the 6-digit code from the app.
3. If the code is wrong or has expired, wait for a new one. **The same code cannot be used twice**; once a code has been accepted, you cannot sign in again with that same code.

### If you lose your phone {#telefonu-kaybederseniz}

> [!DANGER]
> OpenShelf has **no recovery codes**. If you lose your phone and have not backed up the entry in the app, you cannot reset two-step verification from the panel yourself. Before anything happens to your phone, write down the **key** shown during setup (the text code under the QR code) and store it somewhere safe. With this key you can add the same verification to a new phone. Do not share the key with anyone.

Creating a second store owner or admin account lets you keep getting into the panel if one of them is locked out; but there is no button in the panel for resetting someone else's two-step verification. In that case technical intervention is needed (to be verified: clearing the relevant fields in the database). That is why backing up the key is the safest route.

### Turning it off {#kapatma}

To use **Users & security → Disable 2FA**, you must enter your **password** and the current **authentication code**. If you are a store owner or admin, turning it off is not recommended; the panel will guide you to set it up again.

## Users and roles {#kullanicilar-ve-roller}

More than one person can use the panel. Each person has their own email and password; nobody should share a password. There are three roles:

| Role | What it is for | Permissions in brief |
|---|---|---|
| **Owner** | The store's owner | Everything, plus team management and mock mode |
| **Admin** | A trusted senior employee | Managing the store; except team management and mock mode |
| **Staff** | An employee who does day-to-day order work | Viewing and basic order actions |

### Detailed permissions table {#ayrintili-yetki-tablosu}

| Action | Staff | Admin | Owner |
|---|---|---|---|
| Viewing the dashboard and the lists of orders, products, customers, coupons, categories, invoices and emails | Yes | Yes | Yes |
| Viewing settings (secret keys are never shown) | Yes | Yes | Yes |
| Changing an order's status, creating shipments, starting an invoice from an order | Yes | Yes | Yes |
| Adjusting stock | Yes | Yes | Yes |
| Adding / editing / deleting products, categories, coupons | No | Yes | Yes |
| Issuing refunds | No | Yes | Yes |
| Creating, issuing and cancelling manual invoices | No | Yes | Yes |
| Sending email | No | Yes | Yes |
| Changing store settings | No | Yes | Yes |
| Viewing, editing and testing integrations | No | Yes | Yes |
| Integration events, queue status and marketing events | No | Yes | Yes |
| Anonymizing a customer | No | Yes | Yes |
| Exporting / importing data | No | Yes | Yes |
| Reading the audit log | No | Yes | Yes |
| Viewing the Mock inbox | No | Yes | Yes |
| Adding users, changing roles, deleting users | No | No | Yes |
| Turning mock mode on and off, clearing the Mock inbox | No | No | Yes |

### Adding a team member {#ekip-uyesi-ekleme}

Only the **store owner** can do this.

1. Open the **Users & security → Admin users** card.
2. Press **Add user**.
3. Enter the email, name, **Role** and **Temporary password** (at least 12 characters).
4. Save, then pass the temporary password to the person through a secure channel; tell them to change it at first sign-in.
5. If you added an admin or an owner, remind them to set up two-step verification.

| Field | What to enter | Example | Watch out |
|---|---|---|---|
| Email | The person's own work email | `ayse@yourstore.com` | The same email cannot be used twice |
| Name | The display name | Ayşe Yılmaz | This name appears in the audit log |
| Role | Staff / Admin / Owner | Staff | Do not grant more permissions than needed |
| Temporary password | At least 12 characters | (a strong password) | Avoid sharing it in messaging apps; have it changed at first sign-in |

### Changing a role, disabling, deleting {#rol-degistirme-devre-disi-birakma-silme}

* When you change a user's role or password, or disable them, **all of that user's open sessions are signed out**.
* You cannot delete, disable or demote **yourself**.
* The system must keep **at least one active owner**; you cannot delete or demote the last owner.
* Disable or delete the account of someone who leaves the company right away. Their past actions remain in the audit log.

> [!WARNING]
> Do not give anyone more permissions than they need. If an employee only deals with orders, the **Staff** role is enough. The Admin role carries permission to issue refunds, and to change settings and integration keys.

## Sessions {#oturumlar}

When you sign in, a **session** is set up in your browser; that way you do not have to type your password again on every click. [Session](term:session) durations:

| Rule | Duration |
|---|---|
| The session ends if you are inactive | 2 hours |
| Absolute time since sign-in | 12 hours |

When a session ends, you are asked to sign in again; a half-finished form you left unsaved may be lost.

**Sign out everywhere:** If you worked on a shared computer, lost your phone, or suspect your account, press **Users & security → Sessions → Sign out everywhere**. This ends the sessions on **all devices**, including the one you are in, and sends you to the sign-in page. Then change your password.

Technically, the session key is generated randomly, only its hash is stored in the database, and the cookie works only over HTTPS and cannot be read by page scripts. So open the admin panel address only with `https://`, and once live, check that the connection shows the lock icon ([SSL / HTTPS](term:ssl)).

### Cross-site protection {#capraz-site-korumasi}

Every change you make in the panel (save, delete, refund and so on) passes two extra checks: that the request came from the right site (your panel address), and that a secret [CSRF](term:csrf) token specific to your session matches. A malicious page on another site cannot make changes in your name while you are signed in. You do not manage this yourself, but it has one common consequence: if you "pinned" the panel address later and sign in from a different address, you get an error like "The request did not come from an allowed address" ([Troubleshooting](/help/troubleshooting#giris-sorunlari)).

## Audit log {#denetim-kaydi}

The [audit log](term:auditlog) is the journal that shows who made every change in the panel, and when. It is on the **Audit log** page (admins and the store owner can see it).

![Audit log](help/en/audit.png)

| Column | What it shows |
|---|---|
| **Time** | The date and time of the action |
| **Actor** | The person who performed the action |
| **Action** | What was done (see the list below) |
| **Entity** | Which record was affected (a product, an order, a customer and so on) |

The main actions recorded:

* Signing in, signing out everywhere, changing a password, turning two-step verification on or off
* Adding, updating and deleting users
* Creating, updating, deleting and archiving products, categories and coupons
* Order status changes, shipments, invoice requests, refunds (successful and failed)
* Creating, updating, issuing, cancelling and deleting manual invoices
* Stock adjustments
* Updating settings and integrations, testing an integration
* Sending email, test notifications
* Exporting, importing products
* Anonymizing customers
* Uploading and deleting images
* Turning mock mode on and off, and clearing the Mock inbox
* Completing the initial setup

The audit log is useful when:

* "Who changed this product's price?"
* "Who started this refund?"
* "Who signed in to the panel last night?" and whether there is a sign-in you do not recognize.
* Reviewing an employee's actions after the fact.

Values such as passwords and secret keys are **not written** to the records; those fields are hidden. Records cannot be deleted or edited from the panel. The audit log keeps only changes; looking at pages is not recorded.

> [!TIP]
> Glance at the audit log once a week. If you see a sign-in you do not recognize or an unexpected action, use **Sign out everywhere** right away and change the passwords.

## Lockout {#kilitlenme}

If someone tries to guess your password, the system protects itself. The [rate limit](term:ratelimit) and lockout rules work like this:

| Situation | What happens |
|---|---|
| **5** failed sign-ins in 15 minutes from the same email and the same IP address | That email + IP pair is locked for 15 minutes |
| **20** failed sign-ins in 15 minutes from the same IP address (whatever the email) | That IP address is locked for 15 minutes |
| 3 failed attempts from the same IP or 5 for the same email | (If bot verification is set up) an extra [Turnstile](term:turnstile) check is requested |
| More than 20 sign-in requests in 15 minutes from the same IP address | A "Too many requests" warning; wait a short while |

Because lockout counts **the email and the IP together**, someone far away cannot keep you out of your own computer by repeatedly trying your email with wrong passwords: that attacker gets locked out from their own IP address, and you can still sign in.

The same generic error is shown for a wrong email and a wrong password; the system does not reveal whether an account exists. If you are locked out, you see a warning meaning "too many failed attempts, try again later." The wait is 15 minutes.

If the lock does not clear and you are unsure what is going on:

1. Wait 15 minutes; the lock lifts on its own after that time.
2. Make sure your password is correct (upper/lower case, keyboard language, spaces).
3. Enter your two-step code, making sure your phone's clock is correct.
4. If you still cannot get in, ask the store owner for a new temporary password.

Detailed solutions: [Sign-in problems](/help/troubleshooting#giris-sorunlari).

All failed and successful sign-ins are recorded in the database with the email and IP; old records do not count toward the lock (only the last 15 minutes count).

## Secret keys {#gizli-anahtarlar}

Integration keys (the iyzico [API key](term:apikey), the Trendyol secret, the Meta [access token](term:accesstoken) and so on) are the most sensitive information in the panel. If someone gets hold of these keys, they can act in your account's name.

OpenShelf protects them like this:

* **Stored encrypted.** Keys are written to the database encrypted ([encryption](term:encryption)); the master key that decrypts them (`APP_ENCRYPTION_KEY`) is in Cloudflare's secret storage, not in the database.
* **Never shown again.** After you save a key, the field shows only something masked like "saved" ([secret key](term:secret) fields are "write-only"). To change one, you enter a new one.
* **Never sent to the browser.** Neither the storefront nor the admin panel sees the key.
* **Not written to logs.** Keys are scrubbed from logs and error messages.
* **Used only from the server.** Each provider is contacted only at specific, predefined addresses.

### What you need to do {#sizin-yapmaniz-gerekenler}

* Do not share keys by email, message or screenshot.
* If you suspect a key has leaked, **first revoke it in the provider's panel and generate a new one**, then enter the new one in OpenShelf.
* Write down the master encryption key (`APP_ENCRYPTION_KEY`) and store it safely. If you lose it, the integration keys cannot be decrypted; order and customer data are not affected, but you will need to re-enter each integration's key. Rotating the key is a technical process; its steps are in the project's `docs/security.md` file, and exporting your data before you do it is recommended.
* Protect your Cloudflare account with two-step verification too, and reduce the number of people who have access to it. OpenShelf's scope does not cover your Cloudflare account being taken over.
* `SETUP_TOKEN` (the one-time key that creates the owner account at first setup) cannot be used again once used; still, delete any copy you wrote down.

### Secret keys in the configuration export {#yapilandirma-disa-aktarmasinda-gizli-anahtarlar}

The configuration backup on the **Data export / import** page does **not contain** secret keys. So if you restore from a backup, you re-enter the keys; see [Data export, import and backup](/help/data#disa-aktarma).

## Other protective measures (in brief) {#diger-koruma-onlemleri-kisaca}

| Threat | Protection |
|---|---|
| A fake payment notification | The notification's signature is verified, it is re-queried from the provider, and the amount and order match are checked |
| Double processing, overselling | Stock reservations and prevention of repeated actions ([idempotency](term:idempotency)) |
| Card data leaks | Card details never reach OpenShelf |
| Malicious file uploads | Only png, jpeg, webp, gif and avif images are allowed; SVG is rejected; the file's contents are checked |
| Excessive requests and bot abuse | Rate limits on sign-in, checkout, coupons, search and similar; optional bot verification |

## Security checklist {#guvenlik-kontrol-listesi}

1. Your password is at least 12 characters, unique, and in a password manager.
2. Two-step verification is on and the key is backed up.
3. Every team member has their own account and the lowest sufficient role.
4. The accounts of employees who have left are disabled.
5. The audit log is checked once a week.
6. Keys are not shared with anyone; if they leak, they are revoked at the provider first.
7. The Cloudflare account has two-step verification too.

## Common mistakes {#sik-yapilan-hatalar}

* **Sharing a single common account with everyone.** Who did what cannot be told apart in the audit log.
* **Giving staff the Admin role.** Unneeded permissions create risk.
* **Not backing up the two-step key.** If you lose your phone, you cannot get into the account.
* **Sharing a key in a chat app.** If it leaked, revoke it at the provider immediately.
* **Leaving a shared computer without using "Sign out everywhere".**
* **Not having the temporary password changed.**
